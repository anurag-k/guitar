# Metal Guitar Learning Roadmap

## Objective

Become an advanced rock/metal guitarist who understands:

-   technique;
-   the fretboard;
-   music theory;
-   ear training and transcription;
-   composition and improvisation; and
-   the engineering behind electric-guitar tone.

The goal is not merely to reproduce riffs from tutorials. The long-term
aim is to understand the instrument and the music well enough that
tutorials become references rather than instructions.

# Phase 0 --- Foundation Check

Existing beginner experience means there is no need to restart guitar
from zero. Instead, verify that the following foundations are
comfortable.

## Rhythm Foundation

Understand common power-chord voicings rather than only memorizing
diagrams.

Example:

``` text
E5

e|-----
B|-----
G|-----
D|--2--   E (octave)
A|--2--   B (perfect fifth)
E|--0--   E (root)
```

``` text
A5

e|-----
B|-----
G|--2--   A (octave)
D|--2--   E (perfect fifth)
A|--0--   A (root)
E|-----
```

Both are instances of the same underlying structure:

**1 -- 5 -- 1**

The objective is eventually to construct such voicings from interval
knowledge instead of treating every diagram as an unrelated shape.

## Technique Baseline

Verify:

-   downstrokes;
-   alternate picking;
-   basic string crossing;
-   clean fretting;
-   hammer-ons;
-   pull-offs;
-   slides;
-   fretting-hand unwanted-string muting;
-   picking-hand palm muting;
-   controlled bends; and
-   controlled vibrato.

Use BERNTH and Ben Eller selectively when a particular mechanical issue
needs work.

# Phase 1 --- Metal Foundation

## 1. Metal Rhythm

### Power Chords

Study systematically:

-   root + fifth;
-   root + fifth + octave;
-   sixth-string-root shapes;
-   fifth-string-root shapes;
-   fourth-string-root shapes;
-   inverted power chords;
-   open-string power chords; and
-   chromatic power-chord movement.

### Palm Muting

Learn:

-   open versus muted notes;
-   degrees of muting;
-   picking-hand position relative to the bridge; and
-   alternating muted and open notes.

### Pedal-Tone Riffing

Example:

``` text
E|0-0-0-3-0-5-0-6-5
```

Understand the vocabulary behind the riff:

**low pedal note + moving notes/chords**

rather than only memorizing fret numbers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Rhythm and Timing

Develop rhythm independently of distortion.

Progress through:

1.  quarter notes;
2.  eighth notes;
3.  sixteenth notes;
4.  eighth-note triplets;
5.  sixteenth-note combinations;
6.  gallops;
7.  reverse gallops;
8.  syncopation; and
9.  mixed subdivisions.

Use a metronome and prioritize clean timing over BPM.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Lead Guitar

Begin with the minor pentatonic scale, but learn its interval structure:

**1 -- ♭3 -- 4 -- 5 -- ♭7**

Learn where those intervals occur around a root rather than only
memorizing five disconnected boxes.

Progress conceptually:

**minor pentatonic → natural minor → modes**

Technique develops alongside the theory:

**bend → vibrato → slide → hammer-on/pull-off → alternate picking**

BERNTH becomes increasingly useful as speed and picking mechanics become
more demanding.

# Music Theory Track

Progress through:

1.  the 12 notes;
2.  semitones and whole tones;
3.  intervals;
4.  the major scale;
5.  scale degrees;
6.  chord construction;
7.  major and minor triads;
8.  keys;
9.  natural minor;
10. diatonic chords;
11. chord progressions;
12. pentatonic scales;
13. modes;
14. modal harmony; and
15. advanced harmony.

Always translate abstract theory onto the fretboard.

For example, learning that a minor third equals three semitones is only
the beginning. Find minor thirds around different roots on the guitar,
hear the interval, and identify it inside riffs and chords.

A useful free textbook/reference is **Open Music Theory**:

https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Ear-Training Track

Ear training starts immediately rather than waiting until theory is
"finished."

Progress from:

1.  finding the root of a simple riff;
2.  determining whether the next note moves higher or lower;
3.  finding that note on the guitar;
4.  identifying intervals;
5.  transcribing simple riffs;
6.  transcribing increasingly complex riffs;
7.  identifying chords/harmony; and
8.  transcribing solos.

Long-term target:

**hear riff → find notes → recognize intervals → understand the riff**

This is a major part of becoming independent of tutorials.

# Tone Engineering Track

Use the existing rig as an engineering laboratory rather than relying on
downloaded presets.

## A. Guitar and Pickups

Study:

-   how magnetic pickups work;
-   humbuckers versus single coils;
-   pickup position;
-   output level;
-   impedance;
-   frequency response; and
-   guitar volume/tone circuits.

## B. Signal Fundamentals

Study:

-   amplitude;
-   frequency;
-   harmonics;
-   decibels;
-   headroom;
-   signal-to-noise ratio; and
-   dynamic range.

## C. Gain and Nonlinearity

Study:

-   gain versus volume;
-   clipping;
-   saturation;
-   soft versus hard clipping;
-   harmonics generated by distortion; and
-   why excessive gain can reduce metal-rhythm definition.

## D. Pedals and GT-1 Processing

Study experimentally:

-   clean boost;
-   overdrive;
-   distortion;
-   fuzz;
-   compression;
-   noise suppression/gating; and
-   EQ.

A useful experiment:

``` text
PRS
 ↓
GT-1 Overdrive
   Drive: low
   Level: high
 ↓
Terror Stamp
 ↓
V30
```

Compare this against the Terror Stamp without the overdrive. Listen
specifically to attack, low-frequency tightness, palm-muted definition
and noise.

## E. Amplifier

Study:

-   preamp versus power amp;
-   the 12AX7/ECC83;
-   preamp distortion;
-   master volume;
-   solid-state versus tube power amplification;
-   negative feedback; and
-   amplifier headroom.

The Terror Stamp provides a useful real example:

**tube preamp → solid-state power amplifier**

Later, compare this conceptually with an all-tube amplifier such as a
6505 MH:

**tube preamp → tube power amplifier**

## F. Speaker and Cabinet

Study:

-   speaker impedance;
-   sensitivity;
-   power handling;
-   frequency response;
-   guitar speakers versus full-range speakers;
-   Celestion Vintage 30 characteristics;
-   cabinet dimensions/construction; and
-   1×12 versus 2×12 versus 4×12.

## G. Digital Guitar Processing

Using the Boss GT-1, study:

-   ADC and DAC;
-   sampling;
-   digital effects;
-   amp modelling;
-   cabinet simulation;
-   impulse responses; and
-   latency.

## H. Recording and Production

Eventually study:

-   microphones;
-   microphone position;
-   audio interfaces;
-   cabinet simulation;
-   IRs;
-   double tracking;
-   panning;
-   EQ;
-   compression; and
-   basic metal-guitar mixing.

The aim is to understand why a recorded metal guitar tone can differ
greatly from an impressive in-room amp tone.

## Songs --- Application

Real music runs alongside every phase.

Courses and exercises should explain and develop abilities used in songs

-   gallops → learn a song containing gallops;
-   natural minor → identify it in a riff or solo;
-   pedal tones → find them in actual metal rhythm guitar;
-   harmonized thirds → identify them in dual-guitar arrangements.

# Suggested Practice Structure

For a normal **30--45 minute session**:

### Technique --- \~10 minutes

Work on one specific mechanical skill.

Examples:

-   alternate picking;
-   muting;
-   gallops;
-   bends;
-   synchronization.

### Curriculum / Theory --- \~10 minutes

Work on one defined topic from the roadmap.

Examples:

-   power-chord construction;
-   intervals;
-   natural minor;
-   rhythm subdivisions.

### Actual Music --- \~15--25 minutes

Learn and refine a real rock/metal song or riff.

Once or twice per week, replace the theory portion with a
**tone-engineering experiment** using the GT-1/Terror Stamp/V30.

# Principles

## 1. Technique before speed

Increase BPM only when the movement and timing are clean and relaxed.

## 2. Understand shapes rather than merely memorizing them

Ask:

-   What is the root?
-   What are the intervals?
-   Why does this shape work?
-   Where else can the same intervals be played?

## 3. Theory must reach the fretboard

A theoretical concept is not fully learned until it can be found, played
and heard on the guitar.

## 4. Tone presets are examples, not answers

Instead of memorizing:

> "Use these settings for metal."

Learn why a signal chain produces the result.

## 5. Learn complete music

Exercises develop abilities; songs develop musicianship.

## 6. Ear training is essential

The ultimate independence from tutorials comes from connecting:

**sound ↔ fretboard ↔ theory**

## 7. Gear is not the current bottleneck

The existing PRS SE Standard 24 + Boss GT-1 + Orange Terror Stamp +
Harley Benton G112 with Celestion V30 is sufficient for this
curriculum.

Future amplifier purchases should be driven by a specific desired sound
or response, not by the belief that a dedicated metal amp is required to
learn metal.

Begin with:

Study:

1.  what a power chord actually is;
2.  root and perfect-fifth intervals;
3.  root--5 and root--5--octave forms;

5.  inversions;
6.  open-string forms;
7.  clean fretting and unwanted-string muting;

9.  pedal-tone applications; and
